Conjunctive service · EMDR

Conjunctive EMDR

Focused EMDR therapy alongside the therapy you already have.

I offer EMDR as a conjunctive service for adults who want to stay with their current therapist and add focused trauma work. Your therapist stays your therapist. I work with you on one agreed piece of the work, such as a specific memory, a set of memories, or a pattern that has stayed stuck.

EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing) helps the brain process memories that have remained distressing. It can be useful when a person understands what happened to them, and the memory still feels present in the body.

When I offer this

A memory that stays present

A specific event continues to appear as intrusive memories, nightmares, or strong reactions, even after a lot of good therapy.

A stuck point in therapy

The work with a current therapist is going well, and one piece has stopped moving.

Trauma work a therapist doesn't offer

A therapist has identified trauma that calls for EMDR, and the relationship with that therapist is worth keeping.

PTSD

PTSD often follows a specific event or series of events. Once resources are in place, we can often focus on specific memories.

C-PTSD and attachment wounds

These often come from years of not having enough safety or attunement. I usually move more slowly here, and I often combine IFS and EMDR.

Questions

What does conjunctive mean?

Conjunctive (also called adjunctive or concurrent) means this work runs alongside the care you already have. Your therapist stays your therapist. I add one focused piece they may not offer, and with your written permission we stay in touch so the work fits together.

Do I keep seeing my therapist during EMDR?

Yes. Some people continue their regular sessions, and some reduce them for a time. We can decide this together with your therapist.

What if my therapist doesn't know much about EMDR?

That's common. I can explain what EMDR involves and how I would coordinate with them. Your therapist doesn't need EMDR training for this to work, and it helps if they support the plan.

How many sessions will it take?

It depends on the goal. Focused conjunctive EMDR is often described as lasting about three to twelve sessions, usually for a specific memory or stuck point. C-PTSD and attachment wounds usually take longer. We'll review progress together, along with your therapist.

Do I have to talk about the trauma in detail?

No. EMDR does not require a detailed retelling of what happened.

Can we do this online?

Yes. I offer sessions in person in Brooklyn Heights and online anywhere in New York and New Jersey. Online, we use eye movements on screen or self-tapping.

What does the research say?

The World Health Organization lists EMDR among the treatments to consider for adults with PTSD, as a conditional recommendation based on moderate-quality evidence. Clinicians have described conjunctive EMDR as a model, but I haven't found outcome research on this arrangement specifically, so I can't tell you how it compares with receiving all of your care from one therapist.
